# HR Analytics Dashboard

## Project Overview

This project is an **HR Analytics Dashboard** developed using **Python, Pandas, and Power BI** to analyze employee attrition, workforce demographics, compensation, tenure, and key factors associated with employee turnover.

The project combines Python-based exploratory data analysis with an interactive Power BI dashboard to convert employee-level HR data into clear business insights.

---

## Business Problem

Employee attrition can affect workforce stability, productivity, recruitment costs, and employee retention.

This project analyzes HR data to answer questions such as:

- How many employees are leaving the organization?
- What is the overall attrition rate?
- Which departments and job roles have higher attrition?
- How does attrition vary with age, gender, marital status, and education?
- How are overtime, job satisfaction, stock options, income, and career factors related to attrition?
- How do compensation and tenure differ across employees?
- What employee-level information can HR teams use for further analysis?

---

## Project Objectives

- Perform exploratory data analysis using Python and Pandas.
- Identify patterns and factors associated with employee attrition.
- Analyze employee demographics and workforce composition.
- Examine compensation, job level, tenure, promotions, training, and performance.
- Build an interactive Power BI dashboard for HR analysis.
- Present findings in a business-friendly format.

## Business Perspective

The dashboard is designed to help HR teams explore workforce patterns and identify areas that may require further investigation.

The analysis focuses on relationships between attrition and factors such as:

- Overtime
- Job satisfaction
- Compensation
- Job level
- Tenure
- Career progression
- Stock options
- Demographics
- Job role
- Department

These relationships should be interpreted as **analytical associations rather than proof of causation**.

---

## Dataset Limitations

The dataset is a structured HR employee dataset and has limitations for real-world HR decision-making.

Important limitations include:

- It represents a specific dataset rather than a live organizational workforce.
- Some potentially useful HR variables are not available.
- Observed relationships do not establish causation.
- Historical employee data may not represent current workforce conditions.
- Further investigation would be required before making HR decisions.
